Solutions to Exercise: Designing a Simple Knowledge Graph

  1. For each of the following situations, write down an ontology (a set of entities and relation between them) that represents the situation.

a) A Healthcare Ontology.

  • Patients have diseases and symptoms

  • Symptoms are caused by diseases

  • Medicines treat diseases

  • Patients are prescribed medicines

Solution¶

The entities (unary predicates) are straightforward and we need:

  • Patient(p)

  • Disease(d)

  • Symptom(s)

  • Medicine(m)

The relations (binary predicates) are then

  • HasDisease(p,d)

  • HasSymptom(p,s)

  • Causes(d,s)

  • Treats(m,d)

  • Prescribed(p,m)

b) A Manufacturing Process.

  • A product has components

  • The components are provide by the suppliers

  • Fitting a component requires specific tools

Solution¶

  • Product(p)

  • Component(c)

  • Supplier(s)

  • Tool(t)

  • HasComponent(p,c)

  • SuppliedBy(c,s)

  • RequiresTool(c,t)

c) Works of Art.

  • Artworks are created by people

  • Artworks depict events and/or people

  • Events tale place at locations

  • People take part in events

How is this example different to the others?

Solution¶

  • Artwork(a)

  • Person(p)

  • Event(e)

  • Location(l)

  • Depicts(a,{e,p})

  • At(e,l)

  • Created(a,p)

This is different for two reasons:

  • There are parallel edges Depicts(a,p) and Created(a,p) (they might point in opposite directions)

  • The relation Depicts has two possible “destinations”.

Both of these are perfectly normal and acceptable features of knowledge graphs.
